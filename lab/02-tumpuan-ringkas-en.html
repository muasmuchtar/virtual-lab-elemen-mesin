Support field guide

Restraint creates reaction

△ Pin / Sendi

Blocks x and y translation; allows rotation. Reactions RAx and RAy; no reaction couple. A locating spindle bearing is idealized this way in this 2D model.

◉ Roller / Rol

Blocks motion normal to its horizontal track; permits x translation and rotation. Only RBy. A floating bearing allows axial thermal expansion.

▥ Fixed / Jepit

Blocks x, y and rotation. RAx, RAy and MA. Used for a rigidly mounted clamping arm or robot-link base.

↕ Guide / Pemandu

This lab uses a vertical frictionless guide: y motion is free; x and rotation are restrained. Reactions RAx and MA, with RAy = 0. Roller B supplies the vertical restraint. A horizontal guide would have different reaction components.

Why does the shear diagram jump?

A downward point load causes a shear jump of −P sin θ. A full-span UDL creates slope dV/dx = −w. The bending moment is continuous through a point force; an applied couple changes moment.

Try a short experiment

Set w = 0 and θ = 90°. Move the cutting load from A to B. For pin–roller supports, does RBy/P equal x/L? Then compare with a cantilever: why does MA increase as the load moves away from A?

What does “failure” mean here?

The highlighted section reaches first bending yield when |M|/Z ≥ 250 MPa. This is a teaching threshold, not a rated machine capacity. Buckling, fatigue, shear yield, axial stress, bearing limits and nonlinear post-yield behavior are not modeled. Elastic deflection beyond first yield is an extrapolation; the lab does not simulate collapse.
